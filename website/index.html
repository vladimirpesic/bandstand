The place you play from.

Bandstand is a chord-chart reader, practice tool and backing-track engine for working musicians. A real, sampled rhythm section behind chords you already know — walking bass, comping piano, brushes — generated on your machine, not streamed from one.

Built like a band, not a preset

Every backing track is generated part by part, from corpora of how real players play — then rendered through a real sampled soundbank.

A rhythm section that sounds like one

A 148 MB General MIDI soundfont — Frank Wen's FluidR3, the de-facto free standard — played by a real SF2 sampler in a Rust audio core.

Walking bass, from real lines

Bass lines are tiled from a corpus of transcribed walking cells and scored for how well the line walks. It sounds like a bassist because it learned from them.

Comping that knows when — and what

A corpus of rhythmic cells decides when to hit; a voicing engine decides which notes. Two independent knobs on the pianist behind you.

Drums with fills and variation

Pattern-based drums with fills and variation slots, so the groove breathes instead of looping.

Practice that pushes back

Tempo ramp, key cycling and loop practice — the three features that actually make you faster, built into the transport.

The melody on the stand

Written parts imported from MusicXML sit next to the chords — Bandstand keeps what other chart apps throw away.

Page-turner ready

Bluetooth foot pedals (AirTurn and friends) drive next/previous. Both hands stay on the instrument.

Yours, offline

Songs are plain JSON in a local library. No cloud, no accounts, no telemetry, no subscription.

Bring your book

If you already keep your charts in iReal Pro, you already have a Bandstand library. One paste imports a whole playlist — tune by tune, with repeats, endings and road maps intact.

  • iReal Pro playlists

    Paste an irealbook:// URL and an entire book arrives at once. The scheme carries chords, structure, tempo and the playlist name.

  • MusicXML — .xml and .mxl

    Chords, key, time, repeats and the melody, from MuseScore, Sibelius, Finale and friends. The part with the chords is found for you.

  • Plain text

    Type a chart where you are. Bar lines and chord symbols are enough — Bandstand works out which format it is reading.

| Cmaj7 | A7b9 | Dm7 | G7 |

Bandstand is a reader and a practice engine, not a chart marketplace — keep iReal Pro for the editing you already know. The book stays where you built it; this is where it gets played.

The sound it comes with

Bandstand's recommended bank is Frank Wen's FluidR3 GM — 148 MB of General MIDI, MIT-licensed, the same bytes Debian ships. The app offers to fetch it on first run: with your say-so, resumable, and verified byte-for-byte against the digest below before it is ever installed.
